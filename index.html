Research

Two arcs, one thesis: accessible AI must run where its users live

My work sits at the intersection of computer vision, embedded systems, and human need. The world's most capable AI models assume cloud budgets and stable connectivity — assumptions that fail exactly where assistive technology is needed most. I design systems that don't make those assumptions: multimodal models adapted and compressed to run on consumer-grade and NPU-based edge hardware, wrapped in affordable, easy-to-use devices — bringing capable assistive technology within reach of the communities that need it most.

The same edge-first approach scales beyond the lab: the OCR pipelines and NPU deployment techniques from my research now contribute to Bangladesh's digital-transformation efforts — supporting energy-sector monitoring and large-scale document digitisation for public services.

M.Sc. thesis · KUET · 2025

Adapting Large Multimodal Models with Enhanced Optical Character Recognition for Vision Assistants

A wearable vision assistant that bridges performance and affordability: a lightweight capture device performs on-device OCR, speech recognition, and text-to-speech; an edge node runs a local LLM for responsive interaction; and an 8B-parameter open-source multimodal model on a consumer-grade GPU server handles complex visual reasoning. The OCR pipeline — with automatic orientation correction, text sorting, and grouping — delivers reliable reading across diverse real-world scenes, evaluated on assistive visual question answering.

Supervisor: Prof. Muhammad Sheikh Sadi · Thesis no. CSER-M-25-03 · Degree awarded Aug 2025, CGPA 4.00/4.00
